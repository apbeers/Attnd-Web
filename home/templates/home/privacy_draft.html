Privacy Policy

Effective date: {{ effective_date|default:"(not published yet)" }}

Attnd helps people follow live events (maps, schedules, updates and a personal plan) and helps organizers run them. We collect as little as we can. Attendees never create an account, and we don't use advertising, tracking or third-party analytics. We don't sell personal information or share it for advertising.

If you use the Attnd app or App Clip (attendees)

The iPhone app and its App Clip (the instant version that opens from a QR code) work the same way.

  • A random app ID. On first launch the app creates a random identifier that isn't linked to your name, email, phone number or advertising ID. We use it to remember which event you joined, your notification setting, your device type and app version, and which version of the event's content the app last downloaded.
  • Push tokens, only to reach you. Apple or Google gives the app a token; we use it only for the event you joined: organizer updates you opted into, urgent announcements (sent to everyone in the event, because they can be about safety), and silent "the schedule changed" signals that make the app refresh. On iPhone, if you follow the event on your Lock Screen, the Live Activity has its own token, used for urgent announcements and deleted when the Live Activity ends or you leave.
  • Your location stays on your phone. The app uses it to show your position, distances, walking times and "leave now" hints, all computed on your phone. Only when you look for nearby events does it send your approximate position to our server, for that search; we don't store it. Walking routes on Android are asked from Google with the start and end points; on iPhone Apple Maps does it.
  • Your plan, likes and assistant stay on your phone. What you add to My schedule, your likes, your plan and your questions to the in-app assistant are kept on your device. The assistant uses the AI built into your phone (Apple Intelligence or Gemini Nano); what you ask is never sent to us or to any AI company. If you share your plan, the link contains only the event code and the ids of the items you chose.
  • App and device verification. The app uses Apple App Attest or Google Play Integrity so our server knows requests come from the genuine app. Apple or Google tell us whether the checks pass; we keep the result and a key identifier, not personal information.
  • Anonymous usage counts. The app tells us how often features are used ("the Map was opened", "a place was liked") so organizers can see how their app was used. These counts carry no identifier and are stored as 5-minute totals, so they can't be linked to you.
  • Crash and performance reports, without any identifier: the app and system version, the phone model and where in our code a problem happened, plus anonymous timings. Used only to fix problems; deleted after 30 days.
  • Debug logging, only if you turn it on (About & support): for 10 minutes the app sends a technical log labelled with a random debug code it shows you, never your app ID, and never what you typed, your exact location or tokens. Deleted after 14 days. Our own test builds send these logs all the time and keep them 7 days.
  • Camera, only while you scan a QR code; scanning happens on the phone.
  • Images. Place photos come from our servers, or, when no other photo exists, straight from Google Maps (your phone loads them from Google, which sees your IP address like any website you visit).

If you are an organizer previewing your own event on your phone, that phone is linked to your account for up to 7 days so it shows your unpublished changes; it isn't counted as an attendee.

If you visit our websites

  • attnd.app (the home page, join and shared-plan pages): no tracking cookies. To show "Happening near you", your browser asks Google's Geolocation service for an approximate position based on your IP address, at most once a day, without asking for your precise location, and sends us that position rounded to about 10 km; we don't store or log it. We count taps on the home page per day, without any identifier.
  • If you start an event from the home page (the composer), the text and file you give us wait on our server for up to a day until you sign up, then go to our AI import.
  • Event websites built with Attnd Studio (on attnd.site): no cookies and no analytics from us. If a site has a form (for example a vendor sign-up), what you submit goes to the event's organizer, who decides what to do with it; we store it for them until they delete it. "Report this site" sends us your report and the page address.

If you organize an event (the console)

  • Account: your email address and a secure hash of your password (never the password). If you add a passkey or an authenticator app we keep its public key or secret; recovery codes only as hashes.
  • Security: each sign-in records the browser and a random cookie that recognises it (so "Signed in on other devices" works and we can email you about a sign-in from a new browser). Your IP address is used briefly to limit repeated attempts. Session, security (CSRF) and time-zone cookies only; no tracking cookies.
  • Event content: everything you add: details, places, schedules, images, flyers, notifications, colours, layouts, your team and invites, and the websites, files and text you give our AI tools. Changes are kept in the event's history so you can undo them.
  • Ask Attnd (the console's assistant): your requests and its answers, files you attach and short notes you ask it to remember, so the conversation continues across pages. They are sent to Anthropic to produce the answers.
  • AI credit: a record of each AI call's cost, so we can show your monthly credit.
  • Attendee statistics: you see counts only (joins, busy hours, popular places), never individual attendees.
  • Payments (when you upgrade an event): Stripe processes the card; we receive the plan, amount, date and invoice, never your full card number.
  • Emails we send you: account and security emails (always), and event emails such as "your import is ready" or "changes are waiting for review" (each can be turned off in Account).

Service providers

Each gets only what its job needs:

  • Heroku (Salesforce): hosting and the database. Cloudinary: stores and resizes uploaded images and files.
  • Anthropic (Claude): reads the event materials organizers submit and organizers' Ask Attnd requests, to import events, check websites, write suggestions and design Studio websites. OpenAI: creates images for Studio when an organizer asks. Attendee data is never sent to either.
  • Apple: push notifications, Live Activities, App Attest, Apple Music (we look up performers' names to link their artist pages; no attendee data).
  • Google: Firebase Cloud Messaging and Play Integrity (Android), Maps, Places and Geocoding (place names and addresses organizers enter), place photos, Geolocation on the home page, walking routes on Android.
  • Wikimedia Commons: freely licensed place photos (we download them; nothing about you is sent).
  • Mailgun: delivers our emails. Stripe: payments, when used. GitHub: stores our code and crash symbol files (no personal data).

Organizer contacts we research (Prospects)

To offer Attnd to events that could use it, our staff keep a list of upcoming public events and the organizer contact details those events publish on their own websites (with the page each came from). We never guess addresses, never email anyone automatically, and anyone can ask to be removed; we then mark the contact "do not contact" and keep only that. If you ask to claim an event listed on our site, we keep your request and our decision.

Your choices and rights

Attendees can turn off notifications, leave an event, stop following on the Lock Screen or delete the app at any time; deleting it removes the app ID from the phone and our record expires as above. Organizers can export and delete their events and ask us for a copy of, or to delete, their account data. Depending on where you live you may have further rights (access, correction, deletion, objection); contact us and we'll help.

Children

Attnd is not directed at children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect personal information from them.

Changes

If we change this policy, we'll update it here and change the effective date.
